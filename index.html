About

My path here wasn't a straight line: dental surgery, then a Master's in Health Informatics, then two clinical data coordinator roles before landing in a neuroscience lab building imaging pipelines for NIH-funded Alzheimer's research.

That order matters more than it looks like it should. Clinical training taught me what a data point actually costs to collect, and what it means when it's wrong. Now I spend my days writing Python and Bash that turn hundreds of raw microscopy images or MRI scans into analysis-ready, reproducible output — and structured clinical data management (REDCap, Cerner, OnCore) that has to hold up under audit. I care less about being the most technically clever person in the room and more about whether the person who inherits my pipeline six months from now can actually run it.
